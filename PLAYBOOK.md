# PLAYBOOK: product-grounded films with a reason to buy

Updated October 21, 2026. This extends product-taste direction with a commercial question: **why should the intended viewer want to own or pay after enjoying the sample?** Previous playbook preserved exactly in [history](docs/history/PLAYBOOK-before-purchase-value.md). Original technical lessons remain in [earlier history](docs/history/PLAYBOOK-before-product-taste.md). Current tool contracts and safety gates remain authoritative.

## 1. The objective

The viewer should experience a specific appealing part of the actual product and understand a valued situation that buying makes possible. For a book, this can be browsing, keeping, rediscovering, sharing or collecting. For a game, it may be discovery or playing with someone. For a technical product, it may be an actual useful capability.

An entertaining sketch with a cover at the end is insufficient. So is a product-dependent stunt that demonstrates nothing anyone would value after purchase. The question is not simply whether removing the prop breaks the story; it is **what desired experience disappears without the product**.

Do not force an explicit explanation of every benefit. A reader returning to a marked page or a friend continuing to browse can show ownership value. A still or quiet demonstration can work. Entertainment can be recognition, curiosity, material pleasure or real performance, not only a spoken punchline.

## 2. Source and buying situation before script

Use [the brief](prompts/product-taste-brief.md): exact product/edition, audience, current substitute, one buying situation, one paid difference, actual source proof and matched destination. Read [product-class routing](docs/product-class-routing.md), not the entire private company strategy.

Inspect the real material and record a page/card/feature locator plus evidence of inclusion in the sold edition. Select a detail interesting on its own. The fictional ad setup must be labeled separately from the published content. Missing proof is a gap, not permission to generate something plausible.

Write a scene in which that detail causes a motivated choice, reaction, demonstration or reveal. A single source sample may be enough. Preserve the connection to the product after the viewer has received the joke. No universal cast, number of details, twist or scene formula.

Before rendering, ask: is this primarily an attention asset, an ownership/value asset, or an objection/proof asset? A campaign may need different assets; one film need not perform every job.

## 3. Reference: GET YOUR OWN

The [production log](dev/AD_LOG_2026-10-21_get-your-own.md) describes specific book inventions creating another person's curiosity, a request to see the book, and 'Get your own' as both refusal and invitation. This is more than attaching a book to a separate joke: control of the collection matters to the scene.

The transferable mechanism is authentic content creating a desire for further access/possession. Do not mandate the library, two characters, three examples, exact line or 24.5-second runtime. The owner's preference is creative evidence, not a measured sales result.

The openable model mixes editions. Verify each interior against the final sold edition, not a texture folder or date. ## 4. Category-specific paid differences

Books: actual humor plus physical reading, keeping, sharing or collecting. No invented exclusives.

Membership: a real current issue/additional work and a credible recurring promise, or clearly stated patronage. Do not advertise an undefined 'more'. A preview of a separate paid artifact must not imply nonexistent contents.

Cards: collector opening/art is distinct from player interaction. Random packs must not imply guaranteed rare pulls or a complete playable setup without proof.

Objects/furniture: material authorship and real room/use proof. Aura is not permission to hide dimensions, function or fulfillment constraints.

Hardware/kits: actual accomplishment/capability plus honest setup and requirements. Do not depict a preassembled system as an effortless build.

Software: actual input-to-output task, quality and compatibility. No fake interface or unsupported savings.

## 5. Runtime and production time

No arbitrary screen duration, shot count or production stopwatch is the default creative constraint. Read/perform the scene, measure actual voice takes, and allow the audience to perceive the important detail and reaction. Remove filler; do not rush the only product experience. If a real placement requires a shorter delivery, document it and preserve a stronger master where appropriate.

Compact `./ad` still uses configured runtime ranges and execution controls. The existing worker prompts carry product taste, not a new automatic story-duration planner. For unconstrained work, use the authored-film/toolkit route or configure a fresh run after performance timing is known. Never change a running contract or claim an unchanged validator now works differently.

Efficiency means source manifests, reusable rigs/sets/props, accepted voice takes, camera helpers, cheap previews, checkpoints and local corrections. Spending caps and runaway-process protections remain intact. More tokens do not authorize extra tools, paid calls or new production runs.

## 6. Preserve product truth and production conventions

- Original drawings and lettering stay intact; show accurate, readable source artwork.
- Verify edition, variant, price, availability, shipping, signature/bonus and promotional terms before use.
- Default delivery remains 4:5, 1080x1350 unless another placement is requested. Reframe rather than crop away the joke.
- The established early-3D/retro_world character setting is available, not compulsory evidence of quality. A new set is not required per ad.
- Follow configured Frame rules for typography, links and end cards. Actual lettering printed in a book/card is product content, not a forbidden overlay.
- Use verified actions and existing `blender/book2.py`, `blender/shots.py` and rigs where suitable. A legible source insert can replace difficult manipulation without deleting the premise.

## 7. Production sequence

`source + buying situation -> script/performance -> measured timing -> blocked scene/animatic -> targeted correction -> render/edit/mix -> Frame -> creative/commercial/technical review -> delivery`

References: [film build](films/get_your_own/README.md), [workers](dev/WORKER_GUIDE.md), [operations](dev/OPERATIONS.md), [worlds](docs/worlds.md), [library](docs/library.md), [product handoffs](docs/product-content-first.md).

Retain good work. Do not resynthesize accepted unchanged takes, rebuild functioning props or rerender good shots simply because a downstream step asks for a different deliverable. No new agent hierarchy or database is needed.

## 8. Acceptance

Use [commercial review](prompts/commercial-review.md) alongside existing technical gates. Identify the exact source sample, the desired situation, the real paid difference, and where each is supported in the film/destination. Admit what is unverified. Reject concrete false claims and unintelligible scenes; do not reject a good idea for differing from the reference.

Keep four separate outcomes: technical checks, owner creative assessment, audience behavior and attributable commercial results. A script/schema cannot prove humor or profitability. A few specific persuasive pieces are preferable to a queue of generic valid renders.
