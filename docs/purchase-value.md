# Product taste and the purchase bridge

Updated 2026-10-21. Public production guidance. This is a decision aid, not a guaranteed psychological model.

## The missing distinction

A viewer can enjoy the complete advertisement and have no reason to buy. A faithful comic sample establishes taste. It does not automatically establish the additional value of a physical collection or recurring membership. The bridge is a buyer-valued use/ownership situation supported by the actual product.

For an existing fan, 'more of the comics you already see' may be less persuasive than a demonstration of browsing, keeping, sharing or collecting them. For a new viewer, a legible sample may first be necessary to establish whether they like the work at all. Do not treat every audience as already committed.

## Five planning questions

1. Who is this for, and what do they do instead of buying?
2. What real detail gives them pleasure, interest or useful capability?
3. In what concrete situation would they want the product in their own life?
4. What does payment/ownership add that the ad/free alternative does not deliver in the same way?
5. What proof and destination make that purchase understandable and trustworthy?

Do not turn these into five mandatory beats, a scorecard pretending to predict sales, or a spoken benefits list. One scene can answer several. Some campaign assets can answer only one when the others are already established.

## Useful and weak examples

Weak: a book shoots lasers, unrelated characters panic, then a shop link. The prop is central but the reading value is absent.

Stronger: a reader shares a genuinely strange page; the listener wants to see the next one; control of the physical collection becomes the comic conflict. The sample and possession are connected.

Weak: an exclusive Patreon banner over mostly concealed images with no identifiable paid promise.

Stronger: a complete compelling sample from a finished member issue, a clear view of what additional work exists, and truthful recurring terms. The normal public comic remains complete.

Weak: dramatic card openings implying rare/value outcomes that do not exist.

Stronger: actual authored cards shown readably, honest pack contents, or a legal game interaction with a clear consequence.

## Review evidence without inventing psychology

Treat claimed motives as hypotheses unless customer evidence exists. Do not declare that all followers want nostalgia, status or collectibles. A signature reinforces an author relationship for some audiences, not everyone. Do not fabricate customers or testimonials to dramatize social proof.

The scene can be fictional; what it says the product contains or enables must be true. A useful metaphor must not become a false product demonstration.

## Source and handoff

Keep a compact source/value packet beside the script: product/edition, source locator, audience, substitute, chosen paid difference, proof, destination and unknowns. The existing harness carries Taste notes in `creative_directions`, `visual_direction` and relevant `beat.picture`. When manually preparing those fields or an authored-film brief, carry the value reasoning alongside them as plain text. This documentation does not claim the harness automatically reads new files or validates commercial truth.

No additional research sweep, agent, schema or generation pass is required. When the buyer promise is unknown, choose a clearly labeled hypothesis or ask the owner before making consequential claims. Never fill a gap with nonexistent exclusives, deleted posts or limited inventory.
