A texture in a Book Two model is not edition proof. Do not invent unpublished work, free bonuses, rarity, stock, reviews or performance. Do not put private account/customer data in public artifacts.

## Creative task

Propose a small set of genuinely different scenes, not cosmetic variants. Each must let the viewer experience a specific actual appeal and connect it to the chosen paid difference. Explain the connection in one sentence for review, not as mandatory on-screen copy.

Identify what the viewer receives for free in the ad and what remains worth owning/paying for afterward. Reject a random sketch with a logo and a product-centric spectacle that demonstrates no desired experience. A clear sample/use scene can be better than elaborate animation.

Use product-specific wants, reactions, choices, demonstrations or reveals. Keep the original work intact. A complete public comic must not be weakened to manufacture a paywall. Reference films provide lessons, not required settings, cast counts, lengths or punchlines.

## Production task

Choose authored-film/toolkit or a deliberately configured fresh harness run. Measure actual performances and source readability before final timing. No arbitrary seven-second limit or minutes-to-finish constraint. Preserve real placement requirements and existing budgets/watchdogs.

Reuse approved assets, accepted voice takes and source verification. Preview the risky shot cheaply. Make targeted corrections; do not regenerate good work. State which media can actually be inspected.

## Deliver and review

Provide script, source/value packet, storyboard or animatic, technical dependencies, factual checks and final media when rendering is authorized. Use prompts/commercial-review.md. Record technical QA, owner approval and measured commerce separately. Report limits honestly. A pleasing film is not yet a converting ad.
