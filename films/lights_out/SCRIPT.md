# LIGHTS OUT
### An industrial film for Goosnav Book 2, Goosklerf and the Goosnav Patreon
### Blender (Eevee), 1920×1080, 24 fps, about 85 s. Narrated.

**Title.** Automation engineers call a factory that runs with no people in it a *lights-out factory*: nobody
needs to see, so nobody turns the lights on. The Goosnav works is a lights-out factory that keeps the lights
on anyway. It opens with the lights coming on, one bank at a time, for nobody. The title is also where the
narrator ends up by the final shot: quieter and quieter, until he stops talking.

**Register.** A prestige British industrial documentary from about 1974, the kind shown before the main
feature. The narrator is calm, warm, received pronunciation, and proud of British engineering (the
factory is not stated to be anywhere in particular). Over the course of the film he works out that the
factory has no workers, that nobody built it, and that it seems to be making itself. He never panics. He
gets quieter, and the gaps between his sentences get longer. The last thing he says is the shop's address.

**Look.** Raw concrete, sodium-vapour orange, deep shadow, haze and shafts of light. It is shot like 16mm
reversal stock: soft highlights, grain, a little gate weave, and a vignette. The only saturated colours are
the products: Book Two blue `#0033A0` and the three Goosklerf colours, teal `#00B2A9`, orange `#FE5000`
and purple `#330072`. The final shot changes the light to the cold blue-grey of dawn. It is deliberately
nothing like the pastel paper desert of THE DILEMMA: this film is a documentary, not a prophecy.

**Tempo.** The whole factory runs at 96 BPM, which is 15 frames a beat and 2.5 s a bar. Every press, slam
and crimp lands on a beat, so the machinery is the rhythm section of the score. The score is a small
BBC-Radiophonic-style library cue: sine and triangle leads, tape echo, and an organ pad. It is built from
the factory's rhythm and thins out as the narrator loses confidence.

**Voice.** Piper `en_GB-alan-medium`, slowed slightly. It goes through a 1970s broadcast chain: a
100 Hz–7 kHz band, gentle tape saturation, slight wow, tape hiss, and a small dry voiceover-booth room.
The late lines are quieter and closer to the microphone.

---

### 1. LIGHTS — 0:00–0:10

Black. Projector hum. A breaker closes with a heavy clack.

Extreme wide shot, high on a gantry at one end of an enormous concrete hall: conveyors, gantries and
machines vanishing into haze. The sodium lamps strike one bank at a time, starting nearest to us and
running away into the distance. Each one starts as a dull red ember and warms to orange. Shafts of light
appear in the haze. The camera drifts forward very slowly.

Title, small, white Helvetica, lower left: **LIGHTS OUT**, with *A Goosnav industrial film* underneath.

> NARRATOR: This is the Goosnav works.
>
> NARRATOR: It has been running, day and night, for as long as anyone can remember.

### 2. PAPER — 0:10–0:16

Low angle. A reel of blank paper as tall as a building turns slowly, and its web runs up over steel
rollers into a shaft of light. The camera cranes up alongside it.

> NARRATOR: It begins, as the best things do, with paper. Forty tonnes of it, on a single reel.

### 3. INK AND PRESS — 0:16–0:27

The web runs flat under a black platen press the height of a house. Above it, ink falls like rain through
the light shafts: black streaks, hundreds of them, coming down onto a great inking roller. The press
slams on the downbeat, with sparks, a gasp of steam, and the sodium lamps flickering. It lifts. Where
there was blank paper there is now a real Goosnav drawing sheet. The sheets are cut and carried off.

Macro: a slow dolly at paper height alongside the finished sheets as they pass: the Foot Oiling Machine,
the Emotion Nullifier, the Self-Filling Coffee Mug.

> NARRATOR: Ink is applied from above, in the traditional manner. Then each drawing is pressed onto the
> page, exactly as it was drawn.

### 4. BINDING — 0:27–0:39

The sheets drop onto a stack. A blue board swings down over the block. The casing press, huge and
hydraulic, comes down on the downbeat with a boom that shakes the camera and puffs dust into the light.
It lifts, and a finished **Goosnav Book 2** sits there, closed.

Cut to the line: a low tracking shot beside a conveyor carrying finished Book 2s in single file into the
haze, forever.

> NARRATOR: The pages are gathered, and bound in hard covers. This is Goosnav Book 2.
>
> NARRATOR: The staff are very proud of it.

### 5. THE GOOSKLERF LINE — 0:39–0:53

The mouth of a kiln, white-hot inside. Dice tumble out down a chute, glowing, and cool as they fall
from white to teal, orange and purple. They drop into a quench tank, and the steam goes up.

A mechanical card shuffler fans a spray of Goosklerf cards through an arc and snaps them back, twice,
on the beat.

Packs slide into heated jaws. The jaws close with an orange glow and open on the beat, and a sealed
Goosklerf pack slides out.

> NARRATOR: Next door is the Goosklerf line: a card game, with dice. The dice are fired... and quenched.
>
> NARRATOR: The cards are shuffled, twelve to a pack, and sealed with heat.

### 6. THE STANDING ORDER — 0:53–1:02

A tall brass-and-glass pneumatic tube rises through the roof. A carrier with this month's comic board
behind a glass window (a real September 2026 comic) is loaded. There's a thump, and it goes up the tube
and out through the roof. The camera tilts up after it to a hatch open on a dark sky. A mechanical
counter beside the tube clicks over by one.

> NARRATOR: Once a month, a pneumatic tube sends the new comics up through the roof, to the Patreon.
> This is known as the standing order.
>
> NARRATOR (a beat later): Nobody remembers placing it.

### 7. THE REVEAL — 1:02–1:15

The far end of the hall, where the lamps have not quite warmed up. Slow dolly. A heavy industrial arm
is assembling a second arm on a jig. Sparks. The second arm is nearly finished. As we watch, its last
section is seated, its wrist turns once, and it reaches for a part on the next jig.

Pull back. There is a row of jigs, each with an arm building the next arm, running away into the dark.

The narrator's gaps get longer. The music drops out and only the machinery is left.

> NARRATOR: We went looking for the staff.
>
> NARRATOR: There are no staff. There is no record of anyone building the works.
>
> NARRATOR (quieter): That machine is making another machine.
>
> NARRATOR (quieter still, after a long pause): We didn't ask what for.

### 8. THE LOADING DOCK — 1:15–1:25

Silence, then birdsong. A roller door is open on a pale blue-grey dawn with mist. The sodium lamps inside
tick as they cool. On a pallet at the edge of the dock: a Goosnav Book 2, a Goosklerf pack standing
against a Goosklerf kit box, and this month's Patreon comic in a clear sleeve. The camera pushes in
slowly.

Type in white Helvetica, left, one line at a time:

**Goosnav Book 2. $19.95.**
**Goosklerf. Packs $13.95. Kits from $29.95.**
**The Patreon. $3 a month.**

> NARRATOR (very quiet): Everything is ready by morning. It always is.

Behind him, far inside the works, the press slams once more, on the beat.

A white card, set as old web links (Times, link-blue, underlined), bottom left:

`www.goosnav.shop`
`www.patreon.com/goosnav`

> NARRATOR: W W W dot goosnav dot shop.

The lamps click off. Black.

## Facts stated (all from www.goosnav.shop, checked 2026-09-26)

- Goosnav Book 2 (the shop lists it as "Goosnav Book Two (Hardcover)"): hardcover, $19.95, 2025 drawings.
- Goosklerf: a trading card game with dice. Booster pack of 12 cards, $13.95. Kits $29.95, $49.95 and $89.95.
- Goosnav Patreon: $3 a month, a full digital archive with new art added monthly.
- The addresses always carry `www.`.
- The factory, its forty tonnes of paper and its staff are fiction. No product counts, sales figures or
  awards are stated.

## Art rules

- Drawing sheets and comics are the real scans (the 2025 drawing sheets and the September 2026 comic
  boards). They are shown as they are, never redrawn or extended.
- Book 2 is the generic Book Two blue hardcover with Helvetica type. The cards and pack use the generic
  Goosklerf textures made from the flat logo file. The kit box is generic too: plain board, the flat logo,
  and Helvetica type.
- Everything else is built: the hall, machines, robots, light, haze, ink, steam and dice.
